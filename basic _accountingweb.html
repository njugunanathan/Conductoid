Definition

Accounting is the recording of financial transactions.

In simple terms: Accounting helps a business track money coming in and going out.

2. Objectives (Purpose) of Accounting

To keep a systematic record of transactions To determine profit or loss To show the financial position of a business To assist in decision making To provide information to stakeholders (owners, investors, government)

3. Basic Accounting Terms

Assets Resources owned by a business Example: Cash, equipment Liabilities Debts owed by the business Example: Loans, creditors Capital Owner’s investment in the business Revenue (Income) Money earned from business activities Example: Sales Expenses Costs incurred in running the business Example: Rent, salaries Profit Excess of revenue over expenses Profit = Revenue – Expenses Loss When expenses exceed revenue

4. Accounting Equation (VERY IMPORTANT)

This is the foundation of accounting: Assets=Liabilities+Capital

Meaning: Everything owned = what is owed + owner’s share
5. Double Entry Principle Every transaction affects two accounts: One account is debited Another is credited Example: If you buy equipment with cash: Equipment ↑ (Debit) Cash ↓ (Credit)

6. Accounting Cycle 

Identify transactions
                Record in journal
                Post to ledger
                Prepare trial balance
                Prepare financial statements
                 7. Books of Accounts
                 Journal
                First record of transactions
                 Ledger
                Classifies transactions into accounts
                 8. Financial Statements
                 Income Statement
                Shows profit or loss
                 Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet)
                Shows assets, liabilities, and capital
                 9. Basic Accounting Concepts
                 Business Entity Concept
                
                Business is separate from owner
                
                 Going Concern
                
                Business will continue operating
                
                 Accrual Concept
                
                Record income/expenses when they occur
                
                 Consistency Concept
                
                Use same accounting methods over time
                
                 Prudence (Conservatism)
                
                Do not overstate profits
